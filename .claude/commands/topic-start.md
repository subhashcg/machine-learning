---
description: Start a topic — teach it, then check what landed
argument-hint: "[topic name, or its number within the step]"
---

Load the `tutor` skill, then start topic $ARGUMENTS on the current step branch.

1. Confirm the branch is right and up to date with `main`.
2. **Teach the topic.** What it is, the mechanism underneath, where it is the
   right tool and where it stops being one. Ground every claim in code you have
   actually run and whose output you show. Say what is genuinely new versus what
   they half-know already.
   - Demonstrate by measuring; never assert a cost you have not run.
   - Show failures happening rather than describing them.
   - Stop before it turns into a lecture.
3. **Then ask four to six questions** to check what landed. They answer in chat,
   code not run. Test understanding rather than recall: apply the idea to a
   scenario, predict an output, choose between two options and justify it.
4. **Stop there** and wait for the answers.

Do not write the reference card or the exercises yet — those come after the
questions, so they can cover what they actually got wrong.
